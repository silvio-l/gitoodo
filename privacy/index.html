Privacy: website and phone app

This page describes what data is processed when you visit the Gitoodo website and when you use the phone app. The desktop app has its own document, see below. The German version of this page is the binding one.

Hosting

The website is static and served by GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub Inc. (USA). When you load a page, GitHub processes the access data this technically requires: IP address, time, requested address and browser identifier. Legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in a reachable website). Transfers to the USA are covered by the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR). Fonts, images and scripts are served by this website itself; no CDN is used.

Downloads

The download buttons lead to the installers on GitHub (github.com). Only when you click one does your browser fetch the file from GitHub; GitHub then receives your IP address and browser identifier. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The page itself asks GitHub for nothing when it loads.

Statistics with Matomo

To understand who visits the website and what helps them, we use the open-source software Matomo. It records the IP address shortened by two bytes, browser, operating system, device type, screen resolution and browser language, the country and region derived from them, the page you came from, campaign details in the link (such as utm_campaign), and the pages you visit with language, time and duration. It also records events without any content: clicks on download, source code, support and other buttons, a change of language, which sections came into view, which FAQ entries were opened, whether the video was played, that a contact message was sent (its category only), and technical loading metrics (Core Web Vitals).

Matomo runs on our own server (matomo.silvio-und-maik.de) at netcup GmbH in Germany. The data is not passed on to third parties. There is no profiling and no cross-device tracking; it is only evaluated in aggregate.

Matomo sets no cookies here and reads nothing from your device. Therefore no consent is required under § 25 TDDDG and there is no cookie banner. Legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in developing the website and the app as needed. Matomo does not evaluate your browser's Do Not Track setting; you can object using the field below.

Raw data is deleted after 180 days. After that only aggregated reports without personal data remain.

You can object to this at any time (Art. 21 GDPR) by unticking the box in the following field. For the objection to work, Matomo stores the cookie mtm_consent_removed for this website in your browser. If you delete it, you are counted again.

The opt-out field could not load, for example because JavaScript or Matomo's address is blocked. As long as that is the case, nothing is recorded either. You can also object by email.

Contact form

The round button at the bottom of the page lets you write to us. Only when you press “Send” does your browser send your message, the chosen category, your email address if you give one, and the page's language and path to our relay (relay.silvio-und-maik.de). The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR: handling your request.

The relay runs at netcup GmbH in Germany behind Cloudflare (traffic passes Cloudflare's network) and forwards the message by email through its mail provider to our mailbox; your address becomes the reply address. The relay does not store the content. To block abuse it counts requests per hashed IP address for one hour, and it logs only the sender ID, time, reference, result, size and a hash of your IP address with a salt that changes daily; this log is deleted after 14 days. The email stays in our mailbox until the matter is handled.

Browser storage

If you choose a language (in the header or at the bottom of the page), the website remembers that choice in your browser's local storage (gitoodo.site.language), so the home page opens in that language next time. The entry never leaves your device and is needed for this function (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). The contact form stores nothing in your browser except a note that you have seen or closed the greeting next to the contact button (gitoodo.site.contact.greeted), so it does not come back on every visit. Your draft only lives while the page is open. If you choose “Light” or “Dark” in the header, the website remembers it under gitoodo-theme, so the page looks that way on your next visit; “System” removes the entry. It never leaves your device either and serves only this function.

The app

The desktop app is separate from this website. It needs no account, its tokens stay in the system keychain, and it sends anonymous usage statistics without cookies to our own server in the EU: on by default, never with repo names, contents or tokens, and switchable off at any time, already during onboarding or in Settings → General. The details are in the app's privacy document.

App privacy

The phone app

Gitoodo's phone app for iPhone and Android (de.gitoodo.app.mobile) shows you the questions of your own Gitoodo desktop app and sends your answers back to it. It needs no account, connects to no server of ours and contains no statistics, no advertising and no tracking; what the scanner on Android reports to Google is described below. We receive no data about you through the app.

You pair the app once by scanning the QR code your desktop app shows; without a camera you can type in the address shown below it instead. From then on the app talks only to that computer and only within your local network: it accepts only local network addresses, never goes through a proxy, and every request is end-to-end encrypted (X25519, HKDF-SHA256, ChaCha20-Poly1305). Anyone listening on the network sees only ciphertext. The system asks once for access to the local network. What travels is the questions your computer lists under “Questions for me” (with account, repository, number, title and content), your answers, comments and actions, the list of your repositories with the autopilot's state and their running sessions, and a rough device name such as “iPhone · App” under which your computer lists the phone. The desktop app posts your answers as comments under your account on your forge, whose privacy policy applies there.

On the phone the app keeps only three things. The pairing (the computer's address and name, a device ID and the key) sits in the system's secure store: the iOS keychain, not synced, or on Android encrypted with a key from the Android Keystore. The file seen.json in the app's data folder records, for the items it has already shown you, only account, repository, number and when the wait began, so no question is announced twice; it holds no titles or content. And the app view's storage notes whether you turned notifications on or put them off for later (gitoodo.mobile.notify). Questions and answers are only displayed; the app does not keep them.

The app uses the camera only to scan the pairing code, and only after you allow it. The images are analysed on the device and neither stored nor sent. On iOS the system itself reads the code. On Android, Google ML Kit does this through Google Play services; according to Google (developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure), ML Kit sends device and app information, device or installation identifiers, performance metrics and error codes to Google over an encrypted connection for diagnostics and usage analytics. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in a reliable scanner). If you would rather not use the camera, type in the address.

Notifications stay off until you turn them on in the app and the system agrees. They are created on the phone itself; the app uses no push service. While they are on, the app asks your paired computer every 15 seconds whether new questions are waiting. On Android a foreground service with a permanent notification keeps it running for this; on iOS this happens only as long as the system lets the app run. A notification shows the question's title, repository and number; how much of it appears on the lock screen is up to your system settings. Vibration and sounds as feedback are produced locally on the phone.

You get the app from Apple's App Store or from Google Play. What arises there when you download it (account, purchase, installation) is processed by Apple or Google as controllers in their own right under their own privacy policies. If you share crash reports with developers in your system settings, the stores may pass these reports on to us.

“Unpair on this phone” deletes the pairing on the phone; “Unpair” in the desktop app's settings makes it invalid on the computer. Uninstalling deletes the app's data; on iOS the keychain entry can outlast an uninstall, so unpair first.

Your rights

You have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and to object. You can also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority. An email to the address above is enough for any request.
